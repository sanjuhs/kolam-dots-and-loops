What is a kolam?

A kolam is a pattern drawn on the ground at the doorstep of a home, traditionally by hand with rice flour, early in the morning. It is a daily art in South India, especially Tamil Nadu. Similar floor arts are known across India by other names: rangoli, muggulu, rangavalli, alpona.

Dots and loops

One family of kolam starts with a grid of dots, called pulli. In a sikku kolam ("knot" kolam) the line never touches a dot. It curls around the dots, crosses itself, and comes back to where it began, like a ribbon with no end.

How to play

Every dot holds one piece of the line. Tap a piece to turn it. When every strand meets a strand from its neighbour, and no strand is left pointing at nothing, the kolam is complete and fills with colour.

The early levels have just a few dots. The later ones are large and intricate. Levels marked ✦ are classic shapes: full diamonds, woven squares and nested rings.

Rangoli pictures

Rangoli is also made with coloured powder and flower petals: lotuses, peacocks, lamps for Diwali, flower carpets for Onam. Levels marked ❀ are pictures like these, cut into tiles. Tap a tile to turn it until the picture comes back. You can find them all in the Rangoli gallery.

Try it on paper

Draw the dots first, then copy the finished line around them without lifting your pencil. Symmetry is the secret: whatever you draw on one side, mirror on the other.
